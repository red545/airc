# The window: moving it, and when it's in the background

Airc's window has no title bar, so its empty edges work like one, as in Arc.

## Moving the window

Drag from any empty part of the window to move it:

- the sidebar's empty space, under your tabs, and its top row between the buttons;
- the gaps around the page: above it, beside it and below it;
- the top of the page itself, as from a title bar. Hold ⌘ to use the page there instead (select text, drag a link
  out), or turn this off in **Settings › Appearance › Allow window dragging from the top of webpages**.

Tabs, Favorites, buttons and the address field keep their own clicks and drags: a drag that starts on a tab still
moves the tab.

This also works on a window that's in the background, as in Arc: with another app in front, drag Airc's window by any of
those empty edges and it moves with the first drag. You don't have to click it first.

## Double-clicking

A double-click on the window's empty edges, or on the top of the page, does what your Mac's setting says in
**System Settings › Desktop & Dock › Double-click a window's title bar to**: Zoom (or Fill), Minimize, or nothing.

A double-click on the empty space under your tabs opens a new tab instead, as in Arc.

## When another app is in front

When you switch to another app or window, Airc's sidebar dims slightly: text, icons and buttons fade, in a fifth of a
second. The window's colour goes opaque, and its grain lighter. Everything comes back as soon as you click into Airc again.

How much it dims depends on your Space's colours. The dim never makes text, icons or placeholders harder to read than
Airc's minimum contrast (text 4.5:1, secondary text and icons 3:1, placeholders 2.4:1), so a Space with room to spare (Midnight)
dims by up to 15%, a light one such as Sky by about 3%, and strongly coloured ones (Ember, Ocean, Forest, Grape), whose
text is already close to that minimum, by 1–2%. This holds in light and dark Spaces and with Reduce Transparency on. With Increase Contrast on, the sidebar
doesn't dim at all. With Reduce Motion on, the change is immediate.

## Taking a tab into its own window

Drag a tab out of the sidebar and let go anywhere outside Airc's windows, on the desktop or over another app. A new
window opens right there, with the spot you grabbed under the pointer, showing that page. The page moves as it is: it
doesn't reload, so what you typed, where you scrolled and its Back history come with it. The window you dragged it from
goes on to the tab you had open before.

- **Into another window:** drop the tab on another Airc window's sidebar, where the line shows. It moves there, and
  that window comes to the front. Dropped on its page instead, it shows there, on its own Space.
- **A copy instead:** hold ⌥ when you let go. A copy of the page opens in the new window (or the other window) and
  the tab stays where it was. **Edit › Undo** (⌘Z) takes the copy back.
- **A Split View** goes whole: both pages open side by side in the new window. A **Favorite** stays a Favorite; its page
  opens in the new window. **Folders** stay in their window.
- **Changed your mind?** Press Esc before you let go, and nothing changes.
- The new window is on the tab's Space, with that Space's profile, so you stay signed in. Several selected tabs can go
  onto another window's sidebar together, but not into a new window.
- Dropping on a Little Airc window does nothing, and in full screen a tab can't leave the window by dragging. Use
  **Move to New Window** in the tab's right-click menu instead (see below).

## New windows in full screen

When the window you're in is in full screen, a new window opens in full screen too, in a Space of its own, as in
Safari and Chrome. It doesn't float over the window you were in. This applies to **New Window** (⌘N), **New Private
Window** (⇧⌘N), **New Blank Window** (⌃⌘N), a Space's **Open in New Window** and a tab's **Move to New Window**.
Swipe between Spaces or use Mission Control to go back. When you leave full screen in the new window, it becomes a
normal-sized window, offset slightly from the one you opened it from.

Out of full screen, new windows open on the same desktop, as before. Little Airc still opens as a small panel over a
full-screen window.
