The case for a calmer browser

Tabs are not a to-do list. A browser that forgets politely, keeps what matters within reach and gets out of the way.

Mara LindqvistNovember 4, 2026

Every browser ships the same idea: a strip of tabs across the top that grows until it becomes noise. We open, we forget, we hoard. The calm alternative is not fewer features; it is a better default for forgetting.

Pinned things stay. Everything else quietly archives after a day. The sidebar holds your work the way a desk does: what you need now in front of you, what you need often within reach.

The card

Top of Today, under + New Tab. 212 × 112, radius 12, selectedFill with the selected-row shadow so it reads as an object on the theme. Google G 16, title body semibold, copy callout in textSecondary, small primary + plain buttons, a 20 pt dismiss.

One click

After sign-in

ListAccounts gives the photo and address: the avatar swaps in the indicator, Profile menu and footer, with a 120 ms cross-fade. The card turns into the Favorites offer once; dismissing it never brings it back.

Loading rows

The favicon slot shows AircLoadingRing (14 pt, 1.75 pt stroke) only after 150 ms, fading in over 120 ms. The title keeps its full width and fades out at its end; nothing draws over text.
